muvid.footage.scoring._frame_metrics

Pure per-frame / per-frame-pair OpenCV kernels for footage scoring.

Small, stateless functions on numpy frames — the cheap-CPU quality tier + the optical-flow motion primitive. They know nothing about the song grid, clips, or the selector: given a frame (or a pair) they return a scalar / small tuple. That makes them promotion candidates for ``mixing.video`` the moment a second consumer appears (they’d slot into mixing.video’s existing OpenCV footprint with no new dependency) — kept in muvid for v1 to hold the cross-repo surface to one clean audio PR (see the design doc’s federation rule).

cv2 is lazy-imported (muvid[scoring] extra). Frames are BGR or grayscale numpy arrays (as mixing.video.VideoFrames yields).

muvid.footage.scoring._frame_metrics.exposure_quality(gray: ndarray) → float[source]

Exposure health in [0,1] (higher = better): clipping-free AND contrasty.

Two failure modes a luma histogram reveals: (a) crushed shadows (< 16) / blown highlights (> 239) — the clip_ok term; (b) a flat, low-contrast frame (a wall of mid-gray) — the contrast term (normalized luma std). A well-exposed, contrasty frame → ~1.0; a flat mid-gray frame → ~0.0 even though it clips nothing.

muvid.footage.scoring._frame_metrics.flow_residual_and_global(prev_gray: ndarray, gray: ndarray, *, downscale: int = 4) → tuple[float, float, float][source]

Dense Farneback flow → (residual_motion, global_dx, global_dy).

global_(dx,dy) is the median flow vector — the camera pan/tilt estimate. residual_motion is the mean magnitude of the flow AFTER subtracting that global motion — i.e. camera-compensated SUBJECT motion (what the motion-to-beat envelope wants). |global| doubles as the shake signal (the quality tier). Frames are downscaled first (``downscale``×) to bound cost — a hard per-frame budget on the memory-fragile box.

muvid.footage.scoring._frame_metrics.sharpness(gray: ndarray) → float[source]

Focus / motion-blur proxy = variance of the Laplacian (higher = sharper).
